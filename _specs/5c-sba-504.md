# Spec 5c: SBA 504 Loan Structure Calculator

Research done 2026-10-21 by a research job with internet access. Only sba.gov pages (including SBA's own
legacy.sba.gov, where www.sba.gov redirected) were used, all read live on 2026-10-21. The build job has no internet:
use only what is written here. **No current interest rate is included.** No SBA page I read stated a current 504 rate
with a date, so the user must type in any rate.

## 1. What the page is for (plain English)

An SBA 504 loan helps a small business **buy or build the building it will occupy** (or buy long-life equipment)
with a small down payment. A typical deal has three pieces:
- a **bank loan** for about half the cost (first lien),
- a **CDC loan** (a Certified Development Company: a nonprofit SBA lender) for up to 40%, backed 100% by SBA (second lien),
- the **owner's down payment**, usually 10%.

The down payment goes up to 15% for a new business (2 years old or less) or a special-purpose building (like a hotel,
car wash or gas station), and to 20% when both apply. The calculator splits a project cost into the three pieces,
checks the SBA dollar cap, and, if the user types in rates, estimates monthly payments.

## 3. The rules, with exact wording

### 3.1 What a 504 loan is (B1)

> "Long-term, fixed rate financing of up to $5 million for major fixed assets." (page tagline)

> "The 504 loan program provides long-term, fixed rate financing for major fixed assets that promote business growth and job creation."

> "504 loans are available exclusively through Certified Development Companies (CDCs)."

### 3.2 The typical three-part structure (B2, B3)

B2 (SBA lender page):

> "Typically, a project involving a 504 loan includes: A loan secured from a private sector lender with a senior lien covering up to 50% of the project cost A loan secured from a CDC — backed by a 100% SBA-guaranteed debenture — with a junior lien covering up to 40% of the total cost A contribution from the borrower of at least 10% equity of the total project cost"

(A "debenture" is the bond SBA guarantees to fund the CDC's share.)

B3, SOP 50 10 8.1, Section C, "Typical 504 Structures" table (values in percent of project cost):

| | Standard Financing Structure | New Business OR Limited or Special Purpose Property | Both New AND Limited or Special Purpose Property |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Third Party Lender | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| CDC/SBA | 40 | 35 | 30 |
| Borrower | 10 | 15 | 20 |

B3, the sentence under that table:

> "A 504 project has three main partners and generally: a Third Party Lender provides 50% or more of the financing; a Certified Development Company (CDC) provides up to 40% of the financing through a 504 debenture (guaranteed 100% by SBA); and an applicant (Borrower) injects at least 10% of the financing (13 CFR §§ 120.801 and 120.900). No more than 50% of eligible Project costs can be from Federal sources (13 CFR § 120.930(a))."

### 3.3 When the down payment is higher (B3)

B3, "Borrower's Contribution":

> "All Borrowers must contribute at least 10%, which may be borrowed as long as it is subordinate to the Third Party Loan and the 504 debenture;"

> "New businesses must contribute at least 15%. The Debenture will finance no more than 35% of the Project and at least 50% of the Project financing will be from banks or other financial institutions, state or local government, or foundations or other non-profit institutions."

> "Businesses with a Limited or Special Purpose Property: Must contribute at least 15%, in which case the Debenture will finance no more than 35% of the Project and at least 50% of the Project financing will be from banks or other financial institutions, state or local government, or foundations or other non-profit institutions. Must contribute at least 20%, if the Project involves a new business 13 CFR § 120.910."

> "If a Project will finance both a New Business and a Limited or Special Purpose Property, the Applicant must contribute at least 20% of the Project cost. The Debenture will finance no more than 30% of the Project and at least 50% of the Project financing will be from state or local government, banks or other financial institutions, foundations or other non-profit institutions."

B3 also says the CDC may ask for more: "The required Borrower’s contribution ... and collateral ... on SBA 504 loans
are the minimums required by policy. However, in some cases it may be appropriate to consider additional Borrower
contribution and/or collateral to mitigate the credit weaknesses of a proposed project."

**"New Business"**, B3 glossary:

> "New Business: (7(a) and 504) A business that has been in operation for 2 years or less at the time the loan is approved. A business that has been in operation for more than 2 years at the time the loan is approved may be considered a New Business if it is a change of ownership that will result in new, unproven ownership/management and increased debt unrelated to business operations."

**"Limited or Special Purpose Property"**, B3 (the SOP says "This list is not intended to be all-inclusive"). The list
as printed:

> "Amusement parks; Bowling alleys; Car wash businesses; Cemeteries; Cold storage facilities where more than 50% of total square footage is equipped for refrigeration; Dormitories; Farms, including livestock and dairy facilities; Funeral homes with crematoriums; Gas stations; Golf courses; Hospitals, surgery centers, urgent care centers, and other health or medical facilities; Hotels, motels, and other lodging facilities; Marinas; Mines; Nursing homes, including assisted living facilities; Oil wells; Quarries, including gravel pits; Railroads; Sanitary landfills; Service centers (e.g., oil and lube, brake, or transmission centers) with pits and in-ground lifts; Sports arenas; Swimming pools; Tennis clubs; Theaters and auditoriums; and Wineries."

### 3.4 Eligible uses, and what 504 cannot pay for (B1, B3)

B1, eligibility:

> "To be eligible for a 504 loan, your business must: Be an operating business Operate for profit Be located in the U.S. Be small under SBA size requirements Not be a type of ineligible business"

> "Loans cannot be made to businesses engaged in nonprofit, passive, or speculative activities."

B1, uses:

> "A 504 loan can be used for a range of assets that promote business growth and job creation. These include the purchase or construction of: The purchase, construction or renovation of existing buildings or land Long-term machinery and equipment with a useful remaining life of a minimum of 10 years, including project-related AI-supported equipment or machinery for manufacturing products Consolidating debt under the conditions listed in 13 CFR 120.882, paragraph (e) and Repaying or refinancing debt defined as "qualified debt" under 13 CFR 120.882, paragraph (g)"

> "A 504 loan cannot be used for: Working capital or inventory Consolidating, repaying or refinancing debt that does not meet the definition of "qualified debt" under 13 CFR 120.882, paragraphs (e) and (g) Speculation or investment in rental real estate"

**Owner-occupancy (important for a commercial real estate site)**, B3, "Occupancy and Leasing Requirements" (13 CFR §
120.131):

> "When loan proceeds are used to purchase or improve real estate, or when debt secured by real estate is refinanced with loan proceeds: For an existing building, the Applicant must occupy 51% of the Rentable Property and may lease to a third party up to 49%; or For new construction, the Applicant must occupy 60% of the Rentable Property, may permanently lease to a third party up to 20% and temporarily lease an additional 20% with the intention of using some of the additional 20% within 3 years and all of it within 10 years."

> "When the real estate is owned by an EPC: The EPC must lease 100% of the Rentable Property to an eligible OC(s)."

(EPC = "Eligible Passive Company", for example the owner's real-estate LLC. OC = "Operating Company", the business
that uses the building.)

B3 also says: "The 504 Project finances only the costs associated with eligible long-term fixed assets; the acquisition
of any other assets such as receivables or goodwill is not an eligible use of 504 loan proceeds or Third Party Loan
proceeds and must be financed by other means, which may include a 7(a) loan;"

### 3.5 Maximum amounts (B1, B3)

B1 says two different things:
- tagline: "Long-term, fixed rate financing of up to $5 million for major fixed assets."
- body: "The maximum loan amount for a 504 loan is $5.5 million."

B3 (the SOP, the more detailed rule), "Debenture Limits (Minimum and Maximum) 13 CFR §§ 120.930 and 120.931":

> "The minimum dollar amount for a debenture is $25,000. 13 CFR § 120.930(b)"

> "For all 504 Projects, the gross debenture is limited to an outstanding maximum balance of $5,000,000 in the aggregate for each small business concern, including its affiliates, except for Eligible Energy Public Policy Projects and Projects for Small Manufacturers in c. and d. below."

> "Eligible Energy Public Policy Projects $5,500,000 maximum for each eligible project. However, the total outstanding Gross Debentures issued for a small business concern, including affiliates for Eligible Energy Public Policy Projects do not reduce the $5,000,000 limit for each small business concern for other 504 Projects."

> "Projects for Small Manufacturers $5,500,000 maximum for each eligible project, defined as a business with its primary NAICS Code in Sectors 31, 32, and 33, and all of its production facilities are located in the United States."

What B3 says the energy projects are: "Projects for the reduction of existing energy consumption by at least 10%,
and ... Renewable energy sources that generate more than 15% of energy used at Project Facility."

So the cap applies to the **CDC/SBA piece** (the "gross debenture"), not to the whole project. B3: "The Gross
Debenture is the Net Debenture Proceeds plus the eligible administrative costs." So fees financed into the CDC piece
count toward the cap.

**Plain-English summary for the page:** "SBA's share (the CDC loan) can be up to $5 million for most businesses, or up
to $5.5 million per project for small manufacturers and certain energy-saving projects. The bank loan and your down
payment are on top of that, so total projects can be much larger."

### 3.6 Terms: 10, 20 or 25 years (B1, B3)

B1: "10-, 20- and 25-year maturity terms are available."

B3, "Loan Maturities 13 CFR § 120.933":

> "Maturity of the 504 loan is 10, 20, or 25 years based upon the remaining useful life of the property being financed as follows: A maximum of 25 years for real estate; A minimum of 10 years for machinery and equipment; and When the Project includes both real estate and machinery and equipment, the maturity will be based on the asset category that constitutes the majority of loan proceeds."

> "A Third Party Loan must have a term of at least 7 years when the 504 loan is for a term of 10 years and 10 years when the 504 loan is for 20 or 25 years."

### 3.7 How the CDC (504) rate is set (B1, B3)

B1, under "Interest rates":

> "Pegged to an increment above the current market rate for 10-year U.S. Treasury issues."

B3, "Interest Rates":

> "The interest rate for 10, 20, and 25 year 504 debentures is based on market conditions for long-term government debt at the time of sale. Most commonly, the 10-year Treasury Rate is used as a benchmark for the 20-year and 25-year debenture rates, and the 5-year Treasury Rate is used as a benchmark for the 10-year debenture rate. (13 CFR § 120.932) (13 CFR § 120.921(b))"

**Plain English:** the CDC piece has a fixed rate set when SBA sells the bonds that fund it. That rate tracks U.S.
Treasury rates: usually the 10-year Treasury for 20- and 25-year loans, and the 5-year Treasury for 10-year loans. The
bank piece's rate is negotiated with the bank.

### 3.8 Fees (B1, B3), for an optional input only

B1, also under "Interest rates", has a second bullet that reads exactly: "Totals approximately 3% of the debt, rate
may be financed with the loan". The wording is garbled. It seems to describe fees (about 3% of the CDC loan, which can
be financed), but the page does not say so clearly. **Do not show "3%" as a fact on the page.**

B3 lists some fees, for example "Underwriter’s fee for 20 and 25-year Debenture Upfront fee of 0.4%" and "Underwriter’s
fee for 10-year Debenture Upfront fee of 0.375%". For SBA's own fees it says "Refer to www.sba.gov for notices on fee
updates by fiscal year". I did not read those fee notices, so the full fee total is unknown. The calculator should let
the user enter an optional "fees financed into the CDC loan (%)", defaulting to 0, with the help text "Ask your CDC."

## 4. Calculator inputs and outputs

### Inputs

| # | Field | Type | Required | Notes |
|---|-------|------|----------|-------|
| L1 | Total project cost | dollars | yes | Help text: "Land and building purchase, construction or renovation, and long-life equipment. Not working capital, inventory or goodwill." |
| L2 | Is the business 2 years old or less (a "new business")? | Yes / No (default No) | yes | Show the B3 definition in a tooltip. |
| L3 | Is the property a limited or special-purpose property? | Yes / No (default No) | yes | Tooltip with the B3 list (section 3.3), noting it "is not intended to be all-inclusive". |
| L4 | Is this a small manufacturer (NAICS 31-33, all production in the U.S.) or an eligible energy project? | Yes / No (default No) | yes | Sets the cap to $5,500,000. |
| L5 | Existing SBA 504 loans the business and its affiliates already owe | dollars, default 0 | no | Used to reduce the $5,000,000 aggregate cap (standard case). |
| L6 | Fees financed into the CDC loan | percent, default 0 | no | "Ask your CDC." |
| L7 | Bank loan interest rate and amortization (years) | percent + years | no | No default rate. |
| L8 | CDC loan interest rate | percent | no | No default. Help text: "Set when the loan is funded, based on U.S. Treasury rates. Ask a CDC for a current quote." |
| L9 | CDC loan term | 10 / 20 / 25 years (default 25) | no | Help text: "Up to 25 years for real estate; at least 10 years for equipment." |

### Logic

1. Pick the split (B3 table):
   - L2 = No and L3 = No: lender 50%, CDC 40%, borrower 10%.
   - Exactly one of L2 or L3 = Yes: lender 50%, CDC 35%, borrower 15%.
   - L2 = Yes and L3 = Yes: lender 50%, CDC 30%, borrower 20%.
2. `lender = L1 × lender%`, `cdc_net = L1 × cdc%`, `down = L1 × borrower%`.
3. `cdc_gross = cdc_net × (1 + L6/100)` (fees financed in; equals cdc_net when L6 = 0).
4. Cap:
   - If L4 = Yes: `cap = 5,500,000` per project. (Don't subtract L5 here; the SOP's rule for combining energy and
     manufacturer projects with other 504 loans is more detailed than this calculator handles. Show a note instead.)
   - If L4 = No: `cap = max(0, 5,000,000 − L5)`.
5. If `cdc_gross > cap`: show "SBA's part is capped at $cap. About $(cdc_gross − cap) more would need to come from a
   larger bank loan or a bigger down payment. A CDC can show you how they would structure it." Show the capped
   numbers alongside the uncapped split. (B3 says the lender "provides 50% or more", so a larger bank share is allowed.)
6. If `cdc_gross < 25,000`: show "SBA's minimum debenture is $25,000, so this project may be too small for a 504."
7. Payments (only if rates are entered): monthly payment `= P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^(−n))`, where `r = annual rate / 12`
   and `n = years × 12`. Use `P = lender` with L7, and `P = cdc_gross` (capped if step 5 applied) with L8/L9. Show the
   two payments and their total, labelled "estimate".
8. Always show the occupancy reminder: "504 is for buildings your business will occupy: at least 51% of an existing
   building, or 60% of a new one. It can't be used to buy investment rental property."

### Outputs

- A three-row table: bank loan / CDC (SBA 504) loan / your down payment, with % and $.
- Which structure applied and why ("new business", "special-purpose property", both, or standard).
- Cap check result (and the minimum-size check).
- Optional monthly payment estimates (only when the user typed rates).
- Occupancy reminder and the "cannot be used for" list (B1 wording, section 3.4).
- Source line (rule 7): "Rules: U.S. Small Business Administration, 504 loans page and SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective
  October 1, 2026), read October 21, 2026." Each one links to its URL in section 2.
